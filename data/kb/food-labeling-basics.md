In the US most packaged foods must carry a standard set of label elements, and retailers will check them before listing a product.

- The **statement of identity**: what the food is.
- The **net quantity** of contents.
- The **ingredient list**, in descending order by weight.
- The **name and place of business** of the maker, packer or distributor.
- The **allergen information**, covered in its own note.
- The **Nutrition Facts panel**, with serving size, calories and nutrients in a prescribed format.

Claims such as "low fat", "organic" or "gluten-free" are regulated, and each has its own conditions. Some small producers qualify for exemptions from nutrition labelling, but an exemption can be lost if the label makes a nutrition claim.

Before a first print run, have the label checked against current FDA rules by someone who knows them, because a re-label or a recall for a mislabelled product costs far more than the review.

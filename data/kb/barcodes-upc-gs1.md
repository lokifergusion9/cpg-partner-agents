Almost every retailer requires a barcode on each product so the item can be scanned at checkout and tracked in its systems. In North America this is usually a Universal Product Code (UPC), a 12-digit number shown as a barcode.

UPCs are built from a company prefix and an item number. The prefix is issued by GS1, the standards organisation, to the brand when it becomes a member; a brand needs its own prefix and should not rely on a number bought second-hand, because retailers can check who a prefix belongs to.

Each different product needs its own number: a different flavour or size gets a different one. Cases and pallets can carry their own codes so that distributors and warehouses can track them.

Set up barcodes early. A retailer's item setup form asks for the UPC, and printed packaging cannot easily be changed after it has been produced. Keep a table of item names and numbers so the same data is used on the package, the price sheet and the retailer forms.

A consumer packaged goods (CPG) brand rarely sells straight to shoppers. Between the maker and the shelf sit one or more intermediaries, and each one adds a service and takes a margin.

The common routes are:

- **Direct to retailer.** The brand sells and ships to a chain's warehouse or to its stores. This needs volume, a finance team and reliable logistics.
- **Through a distributor or wholesaler.** The partner buys the product, stores it and resells it to many stores. This is the usual route for small and mid-size brands.
- **Through a broker.** The broker does not own stock. It represents the brand to retail buyers and distributors and is paid for the sales it brings in.
- **Direct to consumer.** Online or at a market. It builds a customer base and proof of demand, which helps in later retail pitches.

Most growing brands use a mix, and the mix changes by region and by retailer type.

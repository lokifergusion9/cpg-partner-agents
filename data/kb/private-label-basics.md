A private label product (also called store brand or own brand) is made by one company and sold under another company's brand, usually a retailer's. The retailer controls the name, packaging and price; a manufacturer produces it, often in the same factory that makes branded goods.

For a retailer, private label offers a higher margin than most national brands and a product shoppers cannot buy elsewhere. For a manufacturer, it can fill factory capacity and bring steady volume, but it usually means lower prices, tighter specifications and the risk that the retailer moves the work to another producer.

For a young brand the question is strategic. Private label can fund growth, but it can also teach a retailer to expect a lower price for the same product, and it competes with the brand's own items on the same shelf. Many brands decide to wait until their own brand is established before offering to make for others.

Every party in the chain adds a markup, so the shelf price is well above what the brand receives. A simple way to see it is to start from the shelf price and work backwards.

1. The shopper pays the shelf price.
2. The retailer keeps a margin and pays the next party the retailer's cost.
3. A distributor or wholesaler, if there is one, keeps its margin and pays the brand the brand's selling price.
4. A broker, if there is one, takes a commission, usually paid by the brand out of its selling price.
5. The brand also funds trade spend, freight and sometimes returns or spoilage.

Typical margins differ by category and channel, so a brand should ask each partner for its expected margin and build a price sheet that works backwards from a shelf price shoppers will accept. If the numbers do not leave the brand a healthy profit after trade spend and freight, adding another intermediary will not fix it. Margin and markup are not the same thing: a margin is a share of the selling price, a markup is a share of the cost, so the same business can quote two different percentages.

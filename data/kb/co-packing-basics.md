A co-packer (contract manufacturer) makes and packs a brand's product in its own facility, using the brand's recipe or one developed together. It lets a brand grow without building a factory.

What to look at when choosing one:

- **Minimum order quantities** and run sizes, which decide how much cash a first batch ties up.
- **Capabilities and certifications:** the equipment for your product, and food-safety audits that buyers will accept.
- **Allergen handling:** what else runs on the same lines, and how changeovers are cleaned.
- **Intellectual property:** who owns the recipe, and what stops the co-packer making a similar item for someone else.
- **Lead times and scheduling:** how far ahead you must book a run, and what happens when demand spikes.
- **Quality control:** testing, lot codes and recall procedures.

Get terms in writing, visit the plant if you can, and start with a small trial run. A co-packer that is a good fit can be a real selling point to buyers, because it shows the brand can supply reliably.

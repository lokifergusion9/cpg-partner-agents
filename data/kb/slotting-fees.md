A slotting fee is a payment a manufacturer makes to a retailer to get a new product placed on the shelf. The retailer argues the fee covers real costs: setting up the item in its systems, rearranging the shelf and taking the risk that a new product will not sell. Brands, especially small ones, often see it as a barrier to entry.

Slotting fees can be a one-time payment per item and per store or region, or they can be replaced by free product, extra discounts or promotional commitments. Amounts and practices differ widely by retailer, category and country, and the practice has been studied and debated by regulators and economists.

Questions a brand should ask before agreeing: what exactly does the fee buy, how long is the shelf space guaranteed, what happens if the item is dropped early, and can the money go to marketing or promotions that drive sales instead. Smaller or specialty retailers and some natural-food chains often charge little or nothing, which makes them a good place to start.

Direct store delivery, or DSD, means the supplier's own drivers or its distributor's drivers deliver product straight to each store, instead of sending it to the retailer's central warehouse first. The driver usually stocks the shelf, rotates older product to the front and removes anything out of date.

DSD is common for products that sell fast, spoil quickly or need careful shelf handling, such as bread, snacks, beverages and some dairy.

Why retailers like it: fresher product, fewer empty shelves and less work for store staff. Why it is hard for small brands: it needs trucks, routes, drivers and enough volume per stop to pay for them. Most small brands reach DSD by signing with a local distributor that already runs routes. A brand that ships through a retailer's warehouse instead is said to use the warehouse, or "warehouse delivery", model.

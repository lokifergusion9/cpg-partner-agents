A distributor buys products from a brand, takes ownership, stores them in its own warehouses and sells and delivers them to retailers, restaurants or other businesses. Unlike a broker, a distributor carries the inventory and the credit risk of its customers.

What a brand gets from a distributor:

- a warehouse and delivery network it does not have to build;
- one purchase order instead of hundreds of small ones;
- an existing list of store and foodservice accounts;
- often some merchandising or sales support in its territory.

What it costs: the distributor earns a margin between the price it pays the brand and the price it charges the customer, and it may ask for promotional funding, a minimum order size or exclusivity in a region. Distributors choose brands carefully because every item takes warehouse space, so they favour products that sell reliably and are backed by marketing.

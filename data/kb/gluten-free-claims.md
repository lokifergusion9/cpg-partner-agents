"Gluten-free" is a regulated claim in the US. Under the FDA's rule, a food labelled gluten-free must contain less than 20 parts per million of gluten, and must not be made from a gluten-containing grain unless that grain has been processed to remove the gluten. Naturally gluten-free foods can also carry the claim if they meet the same limit.

The practical difficulty is cross-contact. Oats, for example, are often contaminated with wheat during growing and milling, so a brand using oats needs a supplier who tests for it. Shared equipment in a plant that also handles wheat is another risk.

Many brands back the claim with ingredient specifications, supplier letters, testing of finished product and cleaning records, and some seek a third-party gluten-free certification, which retailers like because it is easy to recognise. Gluten-free is one of the better-known product claims that natural-food buyers look for, so evidence for it can be a selling point in a pitch.

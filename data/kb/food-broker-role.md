A food broker is an independent sales agent for food and beverage brands. It usually never takes ownership of the goods and never holds inventory. Its value is relationships: it knows the buyers at retail chains and at distributors, knows when each category is reviewed, and can get a new product in front of the right person.

Typical broker work:

- presenting the brand to retail and foodservice buyers;
- helping with item setup forms, pricing sheets and promotion plans;
- following up on orders and sorting out problems between the brand and the account;
- visiting stores or buyers on a regular schedule in its territory.

Brokers are normally paid a commission on sales in their territory, often a few percent of sales, and the exact rate and any retainer are negotiated. Because the broker is paid on results, it tends to favour brands that are easy to sell: clear pricing, good samples and some proof that the product moves.

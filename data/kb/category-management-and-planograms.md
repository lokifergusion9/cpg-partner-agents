Retailers manage products in groups called categories, such as cookies, frozen pizza or sparkling water. A buyer or category manager is responsible for the results of the whole category: its sales, margin and shelf space, not for any single brand.

The shelf layout for a category is drawn in a planogram, a diagram showing which product goes where and how many facings (units side by side) each item gets. Planograms are reviewed on a schedule, often once or twice a year, and that review is when items are added, dropped or given more space.

What this means for a brand: a buyer asks what your product adds to the category. A strong pitch shows that it brings new shoppers or a new need rather than just taking sales from an item the store already carries. It also helps to name which existing item yours would replace or sit beside, and to bring sales data that supports the case. Retailers often hire a category captain, usually a large supplier, to advise on the layout, which is another reason small brands should sell the category benefit and not only their own product.

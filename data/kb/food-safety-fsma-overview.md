The Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), signed in 2011, changed the US approach to food safety from reacting to outbreaks to preventing them. It gives the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) more power over most foods, with meat, poultry and some egg products handled by the Department of Agriculture instead.

A food business that makes, packs or holds food is generally expected to register its facility with the FDA and to keep a written food safety plan: identify hazards, put controls in place to prevent them, check that the controls work, and keep records. Rules also cover imported foods, produce farms and the safe transport of food.

Some very small businesses qualify for reduced requirements, and some activities are exempt, but the rules are detailed and depend on what is made and sold. A brand should confirm its obligations with the FDA's current guidance, a food-safety consultant or its co-manufacturer, and expect retail buyers and distributors to ask for proof of a food safety programme before they list a product.

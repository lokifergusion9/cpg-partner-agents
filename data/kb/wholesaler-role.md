A wholesaler buys goods in large quantities and sells them in smaller lots to retailers, other businesses or institutions, not to the general public. It sits between the manufacturer and the store, breaks bulk, and holds stock so that stores do not have to buy full pallets from every brand.

In grocery, wholesalers often serve independent supermarkets, convenience stores and small chains that are too small to buy directly from manufacturers. They offer a wide range of products from many brands on one invoice, and they may run their own delivery trucks and member programmes.

For a small brand, getting listed by a wholesaler can open many independent stores at once. The wholesaler decides which items to carry, and it usually wants to see that the product has demand, a competitive price and a clear way to reorder.

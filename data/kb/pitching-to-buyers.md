A buyer sees many pitches and has limited shelf space, so a good pitch is short and about the buyer's needs, not the founder's story.

What a buyer wants to know:

1. **What is it, and who buys it?** One sentence on the product and the shopper.
2. **Why does the category need it?** What gap it fills, and what it would sit next to on the shelf.
3. **Does it sell?** Sales from other stores, online or markets, ideally as units per store per week.
4. **What does it earn the store?** Price, cost, expected margin and suggested shelf price.
5. **Can you supply it?** Production capacity, lead time, minimum order, shelf life and food-safety paperwork.
6. **What support comes with it?** Introductory promotion, samples, in-store demos or marketing.

Bring a one-page sheet with the item details and photos, and send samples. Ask when the next category review is and what the buyer needs by then. If the answer is no, ask what would change it; a polite follow-up after the review is usually welcome.

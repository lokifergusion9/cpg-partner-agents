The three roles are easy to confuse because the same company sometimes does more than one. The clean way to tell them apart is to ask two questions: does it own the product, and does it move the product?

- **Broker:** does not own and does not move the product. It sells on the brand's behalf and earns a commission. The brand still ships to whoever buys.
- **Distributor:** owns and moves the product. It buys from the brand, warehouses it and delivers it to its customers, earning a margin. It often has a defined territory or a defined type of customer.
- **Wholesaler:** also owns and moves the product, but typically sells in bulk to retailers or businesses across a wide range of brands, often to smaller independent stores.

In practice the words overlap, and the right question for a brand is what each partner will actually do: sell, store, deliver, collect payment, or all four. Brands often use a broker to open the doors and a distributor or wholesaler to supply the stores.

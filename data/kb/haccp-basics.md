HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) is a method for preventing food safety problems by looking hard at each step of how a food is made. It is the basis of many retailer and certification requirements.

It rests on seven principles:

1. Analyse the hazards: biological, chemical and physical.
2. Find the critical control points where a hazard can be prevented or removed, such as cooking or chilling.
3. Set critical limits, for example a minimum cooking temperature.
4. Monitor each control point.
5. Decide what corrective action to take when a limit is missed.
6. Verify that the system works.
7. Keep records.

A written HACCP or similar food safety plan is something distributors, large retailers and auditors often ask a supplier to show. Food made at a co-packer usually falls under the co-packer's plan, but the brand is still expected to understand it and to approve changes to ingredients or process.

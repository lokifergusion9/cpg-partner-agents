Good manufacturing practice (GMP) is the set of basic rules a food business follows to make products consistently safe and of steady quality. In the US, food GMPs are part of federal regulation and apply to facilities that make, pack or store food.

They cover everyday matters:

- clean, well-maintained buildings and equipment;
- hygiene and training for staff, including handwashing and illness reporting;
- pest control and safe storage of ingredients and packaging;
- controls to stop one product, or an allergen, contaminating another;
- written procedures, lot coding and records so that a problem can be traced.

GMP is the base on which a food safety plan is built. Buyers and auditors often ask for a facility's audit results against a recognised standard, so a brand that makes its own product should keep records from day one, and a brand that uses a co-packer should ask to see the co-packer's audit.

US law requires packaged foods to declare the presence of the major food allergens. The original list from the 2004 allergen labelling law had eight: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat and soybeans. Sesame was added as a ninth by later legislation, with labels required to declare it from 2023.

The allergen must be named in plain language, either inside the ingredient list or in a separate "Contains" statement. A product also needs a plan to prevent cross-contact: a shared line or shared equipment can carry an allergen into a product that does not list it, so cleaning and scheduling matter.

"May contain" style warnings about cross-contact are voluntary and should not be used instead of good controls. When working with a co-packer, ask which allergens are handled in the plant and how changeovers are cleaned. Mislabelled allergens are among the most common reasons for food recalls, and buyers treat them seriously.

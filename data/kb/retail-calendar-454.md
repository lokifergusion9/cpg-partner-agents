Many retailers plan on a 4-5-4 calendar instead of the normal calendar. The year is split into four quarters of 13 weeks, and each quarter's three months have 4, 5 and 4 weeks. This makes every month and quarter hold the same number of weeks and the same number of weekends, so a month can be compared with the same month last year.

Because the 4-5-4 year has 52 weeks, which is a day or two short of a real year, a 53rd week is added every few years to keep the calendar lined up with the seasons. Retail fiscal years commonly end around the end of January so that the holiday season and its returns fall inside one year.

Why a brand cares: category reviews, promotion windows and seasonal sets are scheduled against the retailer's calendar. A new product usually has to be presented several months before the shelf is reset, so a pitch that arrives after the review has closed may wait a whole cycle.
